### B3 derivation (INFERRED, from the verified equations above)

**Equations.**
- Tsodyks–Markram (TM), depression-only form with fixed U (no facilitation variable):
  - at a spike, the response ∝ U·x⁻, then x⁺ = x⁻ − U·x⁻ = (1 − U)·x⁻;
  - between spikes, τ_rec·dx/dt = 1 − x.
- Nagel Eq 1 and Kazama–Wilson Eq 5:
  - at a spike, A⁺ = f·A⁻ (or α·A⁻);
  - between spikes, τ·dA/dt = 1 − A. Nagel uses a 1 ms Euler step; Kazama–Wilson write it as an exponential.

**Result.**
1. **Exact for the normalized amplitude dynamics.** With U = 1 − f (or 1 − α) and τ_rec = τ, x and A obey the same recurrence and the same recovery ODE. So EPSC_n / EPSC_1 is identical spike for spike. This holds whether amplitude is read before or after the decrement, because both conventions scale every event by the same constant, which cancels on normalization. Both papers fitted normalized EPSC amplitudes (Nagel: "mean normalized amplitude"; K&W: "% initial").
2. **Not exact for absolute amplitude.** TM amplitude carries a factor U, so the per-event amplitude is U·A_SE·x. The papers' models carry no such factor: Nagel scales the conductance by A; K&W use B(N,p)·q·A. Using U = 1 − f therefore changes the absolute scale by U (about 0.22 for f = 0.78) unless the weight absorbs it.
3. **Not exact if the TM implementation includes facilitation.** In the Markram 1998 form, u evolves with τ_F. The papers' models have no facilitation term. The equivalence holds only in the limit u ≡ U (τ_F → 0).
4. **U ≠ measured release probability.** U = 1 − f ≈ 0.22 (or 1 − α = 0.28) is a phenomenological per-spike depletion fraction. Kazama 2008 measured p = 0.79, and K&W 2009 keep p = 0.79 inside B(N,p) separately from α. So reading U = 1 − f as "release probability" is wrong. As a depression-dynamics parameter it is exact (point 1).
5. **Nagel's own rate form (Eq 3) is only approximately r = 1 − f.** Integrating dA/dt = −r·s·A exactly over one spike gives A → e^(−r)·A: e^(−0.23) = 0.7945 versus f = 0.77, and e^(−0.22) = 0.8025 versus 0.78. With a 1 ms Euler step and s = 1 spike/ms in the spike bin it gives A → (1 − r)·A, which is exact. So the paper's "equivalent to (1 − f)" is exact only for Euler or binned integration and first-order otherwise. Computed in python.
6. **Discretization in Nagel Eq 1 is negligible.** Over 100 ms of recovery, Euler (1 − 1/893)^100 = 0.89400 versus exact e^(−100/893) = 0.89406. Also, recovery is skipped in the 1 ms spike bin. Computed.
7. **Slow-component values are not an Eq 1 fit.** The slow-component r = 0.0073, τ_A = 33247 ms are fits to V_m with Eq 3 (B1e). Converting them to U is a different object from f = 0.91 / τ = 629 ms (the curare EPSC fit).
8. **Fit conditions differ.** Nagel f/τ come from 10 Hz trains and K&W α/τ from 4 Hz trains. Each conversion is exact only as a re-expression of that fit. Whether either fit holds at other frequencies is not tested in these papers. (Nagel says the distinctions were "even more pronounced" at 50 Hz but gives no 50 Hz fit.)

**Verdict.** U = 1 − f (Nagel) and U = 1 − α (K&W 2009) are:
- **exactly correct** for TM depression-only dynamics of normalized amplitude, with τ_rec = τ;
- **wrong** if interpreted as release probability;
- **approximate** when mapping into Nagel's continuous-rate Eq 3 unless integrated with 1 ms Euler steps;
- **not valid** if the TM variant has facilitation.
